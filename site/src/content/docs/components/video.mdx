By default a video autoplays, muted and looping, at 16:9 — the background
video most sites use. `variant` sets the ratio: `2-1`, `16-9`, `3-2`, `5-4`,
`1-1`, `4-5`, `2-3`, `custom` (with `ratio`), or `auto`.

## Loading and playback

An autoplaying video downloads nothing until it scrolls into view, plays while
it is on screen and pauses when it leaves — a page of videos costs only the
ones being watched. Give it a `poster`: that is what shows until then, and
what shows without JavaScript.

`desktopOnly` keeps a heavy background video off phones and tablets: below
the `large` breakpoint the poster stands alone and nothing downloads.

## The pause button

Moving content that plays for more than five seconds must be pausable (WCAG
2.2.2), so an autoplaying video shows a pause button. `pausePosition` puts it
in any of nine places:

```astro
<Video src="/video/demo.webm" pausePosition="top-left" />
```

`top-left`, `top`, `top-right`, `left`, `center`, `right`, `bottom-left`,
`bottom` and `bottom-right` (default). With reduced motion the video starts
paused. `pauseButton={false}` removes the button — for a video that stops
within five seconds, and for a background video.

## Background video

A video behind a section's content goes in `Section`'s `background` slot,
where it fills the section. It has no play/pause button unless the site's
owner asks for one — ask, and the answer defaults to no:

```astro
<Section theme="dark">
  <Video
    slot="background"
    src="/video/demo.webm"
    poster="/video/demo.jpg"
    pauseButton={false}
  />
  <Heading>Built to last</Heading>
</Section>
```

A looping video with no pause control doesn't meet WCAG 2.2.2 for visitors
who haven't asked their device for reduced motion, so say so when asking.
With the button, it stays clickable over the background.
